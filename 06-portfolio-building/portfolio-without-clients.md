# Building Proof Without Paid Clients

No paid gigs yet? You can still build a portfolio a client would trust. Proof doesn't require a client — it requires evidence of real, applied skill.

## 1. Home-lab write-ups

Set up a home lab (free tools: VirtualBox/VMware, vulnerable-by-design VMs like those on VulnHub, or TryHackMe/HackTheBox environments). Document what you did using the [write-up template](./writeup-template.md) and publish to GitHub.

## 2. CTF results

Consistent participation on TryHackMe, HackTheBox, or CTFtime is verifiable, ongoing proof of skill — see the [CTF & cert showcase guide](./ctf-cert-showcase-guide.md).

## 3. A free, scoped review — with clear permission

Offer one free, tightly scoped review to a friend's or family member's small business (e.g. a basic external footprint review, not an intrusive test). Get written permission first, keep scope narrow, and publish the sanitized findings and remediation advice as a case-study write-up (with their consent, and without exposing sensitive details).

## 4. Contribute to open-source security tools

Even small contributions (documentation, bug reports, minor fixes) to known open-source security tools are visible, checkable proof of engagement with the field.

## 5. Teach what you're learning

Write short explainer posts (LinkedIn, Medium, Hashnode, DEV.to) breaking down a public incident or a concept you just learned. This builds your personal brand and search visibility simultaneously — and it's evidence you understand the material, not just that you hold a certificate.

## What this replaces (and what it doesn't)

This builds credibility and visibility — it is not a substitute for the specific permissions and legal safeguards required for real client work once you start taking paid engagements. Review [contract basics](../04-proposals-pricing/contract-basics.md) before your first paid project.
