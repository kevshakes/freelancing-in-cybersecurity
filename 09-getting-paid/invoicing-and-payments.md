# Invoicing and Getting Paid

Landing the client is only half the job. Getting paid reliably, especially across borders, is where many freelancers lose time and money to fees or delays.

## Invoicing basics

Every invoice needs:

- Your name/business name and contact details
- Client name and company
- Invoice number and date
- Description of the service delivered, matching the agreed scope
- Amount, currency, and due date
- Payment method and details (account, payment link, or wallet)

Send the invoice as soon as the work is delivered or the milestone is hit, not days later. Late invoicing trains clients to pay late.

## Payment methods for cross-border clients (relevant for Kenya-based freelancers)

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| Wise | Widely used for receiving USD/GBP/EUR, generally lower fees than a direct bank wire, has local Kenyan bank withdrawal |
| Payoneer | Common on freelance platforms, supports receiving from clients directly and from platform payouts |
| Direct bank wire (SWIFT) | Works but often has higher fees and slower settlement (3-5 business days); confirm who covers wire fees before invoicing |
| PayPal | Widely known by clients, but currency conversion and withdrawal fees in Kenya can be high; confirm rates before relying on it |
| M-Pesa (via an intermediary like Wise or a payment aggregator) | Useful for the final leg once funds land in a linked account; M-Pesa itself does not receive international payments directly |

Compare actual fees and exchange rates before choosing a default method — they change, so check current rates rather than assuming.

## Pricing in USD vs. local currency

- For international direct clients, quoting in USD is standard and protects you from local currency depreciation
- For local/regional clients, quoting in KES (or the client's local currency) may be expected and reduces their conversion friction
- State the currency explicitly on every quote and invoice — an unspecified "$500" or "500" is a common source of disputes

## Getting payment terms agreed upfront

- Milestone payments for larger projects (e.g. 50% upfront, 50% on delivery) reduce your risk on unpaid work
- For smaller, fast-turnaround services, full payment upfront or on delivery is reasonable to request
- Put payment terms in the same document as scope — see [contract basics](../04-proposals-pricing/contract-basics.md)

## If a client is late paying

1. Send a polite, direct reminder on the due date, not weeks later
2. Follow up again after 7 days with a firm but professional tone
3. Pause further work if payment is significantly overdue and unresponsive
4. For platform work, use the platform's payment protection/dispute process before escalating elsewhere
