# Objection-Handling Scripts

Direct clients raise objections platform clients rarely do — no reviews to point to, unfamiliarity with security services, budget uncertainty. Prepare responses in advance.

## "We've never worked with a freelancer for security before."

"That's common, especially for [specific service] — most companies your size don't have a full-time need for it. I can start with a smaller, scoped engagement so you can see how I work before committing to anything larger."

## "How do we know you're legitimate / qualified?"

"Fair question. Here's my [certification/portfolio/writeup link], and I'm happy to do a short scoped starter project first so you can evaluate the work directly before a larger commitment."

*(Always have your Featured LinkedIn section and GitHub write-ups ready to send in this moment.)*

## "We need to think about it" / goes quiet after your proposal

Don't chase immediately. Follow up once, 4-5 days later, with something new — not just "just checking in":

"Wanted to share this in case it's useful while you're deciding — [relevant article, a quick tip related to their situation, or a case study]. Happy to answer any questions whenever you're ready."

## "Can you start today / this is urgent"

Assess honestly before agreeing. If real: "I can prioritize this — here's what I can realistically deliver by [specific time] given the scope." Never overpromise on incident response timelines you can't actually meet.

## "We want ongoing free updates/support after the project"

"Happy to include [specific, limited scope — e.g. 2 weeks of post-delivery support] as part of this engagement. Ongoing support beyond that would be a separate retainer — I can put together options if that's of interest."

## "Send us a sample report / free audit first"

Distinguish between a reasonable ask and scope creep:
- Reasonable: sharing a **sanitized** sample from past work (with prior client permission) or a portfolio write-up
- Scope creep: doing real, unpaid work on their actual systems — decline clearly: "I don't do unpaid trial work on live systems, but I'm glad to share a sanitized sample from a past project."

## General principle

Every objection response should do two things: acknowledge the concern genuinely, and offer a concrete next step that protects your time and rate. Avoid over-explaining or apologizing for having a process.
