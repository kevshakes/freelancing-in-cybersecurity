# Rate Negotiation Guide

Direct clients negotiate more than platform clients — there's no platform-set price anchor. Knowing how to hold your rate (or flex it deliberately) is a core skill, not an afterthought.

## Before the conversation

- Know your number *and* your floor — the lowest you'd genuinely accept for this scope, decided before you're in the conversation, not during it
- Know the client's likely budget range if you can (company size, industry, project urgency are all signals)
- Decide in advance which non-price terms you could trade instead of dropping rate (see below)

## When a client pushes back on price

| Their move | Your response approach |
|---|---|
| "That's above our budget" | Ask what budget they had in mind — don't drop your number first. Then decide if a reduced-scope version fits their budget, rather than the same scope at a lower price. |
| "Can you do it for less?" | "I can adjust scope to fit a different budget — here's what a smaller starter engagement would look like." Never just say yes to a flat discount. |
| "We usually pay [lower rate]" | "That's helpful context — for the scope we discussed, here's what's included at my rate." Restate value, don't apologize for your price. |
| Silence after you state your rate | Let it sit. Don't fill silence by discounting yourself. |

## Trade instead of discount

If you do want to move on price, trade for something of equal value instead of giving it away:

- A longer engagement or retainer instead of a one-off project
- A case study / testimonial rights in exchange for a reduced introductory rate
- Faster payment terms (e.g. 50% upfront) in exchange for a small discount
- A slightly reduced scope, clearly redefined — never the same scope at a lower price

## Phrases that hold your position without sounding rigid

- "I want this to work for both of us — let's look at what fits your budget within a smaller scope."
- "My rate reflects [specific value — certification, track record, turnaround]. Happy to explore what's included at a different budget."
- "I don't discount the rate, but I'm flexible on scope and timeline — let's find what fits."

## Red flags in negotiation

- A client who negotiates hard before you've even scoped the work — often signals scope creep later
- "We'll pay more on the next project" — treat this as goodwill, not a commitment
- Refusal to put agreed terms in writing after a negotiation — always confirm final terms via email or contract
