# Scope Creep & Boundaries

Direct clients (more than platform clients) will quietly expand scope over the course of a project. Protecting your boundaries is part of running a sustainable freelance practice, not being difficult.

## Signs of scope creep

- "While you're in there, can you also check..." (mid-project, unscoped additions)
- "This should be quick" attached to a new, unscoped request
- Requests routed informally (WhatsApp, casual calls) instead of through your agreed process
- A single audit slowly turning into ongoing free monitoring

## How to respond in the moment

```
"Happy to take a look at that — it's outside what we scoped originally,
so let me put together a quick add-on estimate before I start."
```

This does three things: stays warm, doesn't do unpaid work reflexively, and creates a paper trail.

## Preventing it before it starts

- Put scope in writing before work begins (see [contract basics](../04-proposals-pricing/contract-basics.md))
- Define explicitly what's *out* of scope, not just what's in — ambiguity is where creep lives
- Set a communication channel and response-time expectation upfront (e.g. "I respond to project requests via email within 24 hours")

## Boundaries that protect your rate and time

- Decide your working hours and stick to them — "urgent" requests outside scope don't require an instant reply
- Don't do free "just one more thing" fixes — log them, price them, offer as an add-on
- If a client repeatedly pushes scope without paying for it, that's information about the relationship, not just the project — factor it into whether you continue working with them

## A simple boundary-setting phrase for recurring offenders

"I want to keep delivering good work for you — for that to keep working, new requests need to go through a quick scoping step so we're both clear on time and cost. I'll send a short estimate for this one."
