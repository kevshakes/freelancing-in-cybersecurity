# Top Cybersecurity Certifications — Overview

A certification is a fast way to prove skill to a client who can't otherwise verify your work. Pick based on your specialty and stage — you don't need all of these, and stacking irrelevant certs doesn't help positioning.

## Foundational / entry-level

| Certification | Issuer | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| CompTIA Security+ | CompTIA | Broad foundation, widely recognized by non-technical clients |
| Google Cybersecurity Certificate | Google | Entry-level, strong for career-switchers, includes hands-on labs |
| Certified in Cybersecurity (CC) | ISC2 | Free/low-cost entry credential from a respected body |

## Offensive security / technical specialist

| Certification | Issuer | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) | EC-Council | Recognized name for pentesting/red-team positioning |
| OSCP (Offensive Security Certified Professional) | OffSec | Highly respected, hands-on, strong signal for serious pentest work |
| GIAC certifications (GSEC, GCIH, GPEN, etc.) | SANS/GIAC | Deep, respected technical specialization — higher cost |

## Cloud security

| Certification | Issuer | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| AWS Certified Security – Specialty | AWS | Cloud security consulting, especially for AWS-heavy clients |
| Microsoft Certified: Security Operations Analyst Associate | Microsoft | Azure/Microsoft-stack environments |
| Huawei HCIP-Security | Huawei | Relevant for clients on Huawei infrastructure, common in some African markets |

## Governance, risk & compliance (GRC) — see the ISACA guide for detail

ISACA is the leading body here — CISA, CISM, CRISC, CGEIT, and CDPSE cover audit, management, risk, governance, and privacy respectively. See [`isaca-certifications-guide.md`](./isaca-certifications-guide.md) for the full breakdown, including the newly launched **CCOA**.

## Advanced / management track

| Certification | Issuer | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| CISSP | ISC2 | Senior/consulting-level generalist credential, widely recognized globally |
| CISM | ISACA | Security management and governance track |

## How to choose

1. Match the cert to the service you're positioning around (don't get CISSP if you're freelancing hands-on pentests — OSCP signals harder there).
2. Weigh cost and time against how directly it maps to the clients you want.
3. List "in progress" certifications too — momentum is a positive signal, not something to hide.
4. Always link to the issuer's official verification page where one exists.
