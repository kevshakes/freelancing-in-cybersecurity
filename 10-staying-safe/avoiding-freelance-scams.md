# Avoiding Freelance Scams

Tonight's session is about defending clients from AI-powered scams. The same environment creates scams targeting freelancers directly. Recognize these before they cost you time, work, or money.

## The overpayment / fake check scam

A "client" sends payment for more than the agreed amount (often via a check, wire, or payment that later reverses) and asks you to refund the difference, sometimes to a different account. By the time the original payment is reversed or found to be fraudulent, your refund is gone.

**Response:** never refund an overpayment before the original payment has fully cleared and settled, which can take days even when it initially shows as received. Treat any unsolicited overpayment as a red flag, not good luck.

## The advance-fee / pay-to-work scam

A "client" asks you to pay for software, a background check, training materials, or a security deposit before they'll assign paid work.

**Response:** legitimate clients do not require you to pay them to start working. Decline and move on.

## The fake job / data harvesting scam

A posting or message asks for extensive personal information (ID numbers, bank details, full address) very early, before any real scoping conversation, often framed as "onboarding."

**Response:** share only what's needed to invoice and deliver (name, payment details, contract terms) once a real engagement is agreed. Be cautious with ID/passport numbers unless the engagement genuinely requires it and the client is verifiable.

## The unpaid "test project" scam

A "client" asks for a full, real deliverable framed as a test or trial, then disappears once it's delivered.

**Response:** see the scope-creep and boundaries guidance in [03-negotiation](../03-negotiation/scope-creep-and-boundaries.md) — a sanitized portfolio sample is reasonable; a full, real deliverable for free is not.

## The impersonation scam

Someone contacts you claiming to represent a company or recruiter you recognize, using AI-generated or lightly modified branding, to build fast trust before pitching a scam.

**Response:** verify independently — check the company's real domain and contact channels rather than replying through whatever channel they initiated contact on.

## General checks before engaging a new direct client

- Does the company have a real, findable online presence (website, LinkedIn, registration)?
- Does the person contacting you have a real, consistent profile and history?
- Are they rushing you to skip scoping, contracts, or normal payment terms?
- Would you be comfortable if this exact request came from someone you'd never heard of? (If not, slow down.)

## If something feels off

Trust the hesitation. Ask direct questions, verify independently, and it is always acceptable to decline or delay a suspicious engagement, even if it means walking away from a lead.
