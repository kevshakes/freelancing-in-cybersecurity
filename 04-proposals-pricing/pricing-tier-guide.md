# Pricing Tier Guide

There's no single "right" rate — but there is a wrong one if it doesn't match your stage. Use this as a starting reference, not a rulebook. Adjust for your local market and platform.

## Starter — $15–$35/hr (or equivalent fixed-price)

**When you're here:** 0–5 completed gigs, building your first reviews.

**What to take:** smaller scoped audits, single-service gigs (one phishing simulation campaign, one small compliance checklist review).

**Goal:** reviews and testimonials, not maximum income. Every completed project here is portfolio material.

## Growth — $40–$80/hr

**When you're here:** a specialized skill plus a visible portfolio (writeups, certs, 5+ completed projects).

**What to take:** retainer clients, recurring SOC monitoring, multi-week engagements.

**Goal:** consistency and repeat clients. This is where most sustainable freelance income sits.

## Expert — $90+/hr or fixed-fee project pricing

**When you're here:** niche authority (AI red-teaming, compliance frameworks, a recognized specialty) and a track record clients can verify.

**What to take:** fixed-fee projects scoped by deliverable, not hours. Retainers priced on value, not time.

**Goal:** value-based pricing — the client is paying for the outcome and your judgment, not your hours.

## A note on fixed-price vs. hourly

- Fixed-price protects you once you can estimate scope accurately — you're rewarded for efficiency.
- Hourly is safer when starting out or when scope is genuinely unpredictable (e.g. incident response).
- Never quote hourly without an estimated total — clients want to know the ballpark before committing.

## Common pricing mistakes

- Under-pricing to "win the client" — it signals inexperience and attracts scope creep
- No stated rate ("negotiable") — always state a number, even if you'd flex on it privately
- Pricing identically across very different scopes — a one-page compliance checklist and a full audit are not the same service
