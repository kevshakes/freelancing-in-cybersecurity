# Contract Basics for Cybersecurity Freelancers

You're handling client systems and sensitive findings — a scope-and-terms document isn't optional, even for small gigs. This is educational guidance, not legal advice; for high-value or high-risk engagements, have a lawyer review your standard contract.

## What every engagement needs in writing

1. **Scope of work** — exactly what's being tested/reviewed/delivered, and what's explicitly out of scope
2. **Authorization** — written permission to test the systems in scope (critical for any pentest or red-team work — never test without this)
3. **Timeline** — start date, delivery date, and what happens if either side needs to extend
4. **Payment terms** — rate, milestones (if any), payment method, and what happens on late payment
5. **Confidentiality** — how client data and findings will be handled and stored, and for how long
6. **Liability limits** — especially important for testing work where something could break or trigger downtime
7. **Ownership** — who owns the final report and any tools/scripts built during the engagement

## Platform contracts vs. direct contracts

- **On-platform (Upwork, Fiverr):** the platform's terms cover payment protection, but you should still document scope in writing within the platform messaging or a shared doc.
- **Direct clients:** you need your own contract template. A simple one-page scope + terms document is far better than nothing.

## Red flags to negotiate or walk away from

- No written authorization for testing work
- "We'll figure out scope as we go"
- Payment terms that are vague or "after we're happy with results"
- Requests to test systems you can't confirm the client owns or controls
- Unlimited liability with no cap, especially for junior freelancers

## A simple authorization-to-test clause (adapt as needed)

```
[Client name] authorizes [Your name] to perform [specific testing type]
on [specific systems/domains/IP ranges], between [start date] and [end date].
This authorization does not extend to any system, network, or asset
outside the scope listed above.
```
