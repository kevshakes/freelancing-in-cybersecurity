# Positioning Yourself for AI-Era Clients

Clients aren't just buying "cybersecurity" anymore — they're buying protection from very specific, very current threats. Position accordingly.

## 1. Pick a lane, not a laundry list

Generalist profiles ("I do pentesting, cloud security, GRC, and awareness training") read as unfocused. Pick the 1–2 services you can speak to with the most confidence and lead with those. You can still offer others — just don't headline them all.

## 2. Speak to the threat, not just the skill

| Generic | AI-era positioned |
|---|---|
| "Phishing simulation services" | "Phishing simulations that reflect real AI-generated attack patterns — not 2019 templates" |
| "Security audits" | "Security audits that check whether your team can spot a deepfake CEO call" |
| "SOC monitoring" | "Remote SOC monitoring built for teams too small for a 24/7 in-house team" |

## 3. Use the BEFORE / AFTER framework

**Before:** vague, adjective-heavy, no proof.
*"Cybersecurity enthusiast. Fast learner, hard worker, looking for opportunities."*

**After:** specific problem, specific proof, specific outcome.
*"I help SMEs stop AI-driven phishing before it reaches an inbox — 3 years, AWS Security Specialty certified, 12 completed audits."*

Rewrite your own headline using this framework before you touch a platform profile.

## 4. Your personal brand is due diligence, not vanity

Before a client messages you, they Google you. What should they find?

- A GitHub with at least one write-up or project
- A LinkedIn that's active in the last 30 days
- Certifications listed consistently across platforms
- No outdated or contradictory information between your resume, LinkedIn, and platform profiles

## 5. Niche examples worth considering

- AI red-teaming / chatbot abuse testing
- Deepfake and voice-clone awareness training
- Compliance readiness for AI tool adoption (data privacy, GDPR-adjacent)
- SOC-as-a-service for startups under 50 employees
- Incident response retainers for e-commerce/SMB
